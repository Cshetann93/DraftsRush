Screenplay Format Guide: The Industry Standard Specification

Hollywood script readers, coverage executives, and contest judges discard screenplays that violate standard margin geometry within 30 seconds. Master the strict mechanical requirements of 12-point Courier, tab indentations, sluglines, and all 16 technical screenplay elements.

By Lead Writer: CHETAN • UPDATED FOR 2026 • 15 MIN READ
TL;DR // Quick Answer

Standard Screenplay Format Rules

Standard screenplay format requires 12-point Courier font typed on 8.5 x 11 inch paper with a 1.5-inch left margin (for three-hole punching) and 1.0-inch top, bottom, and right margins, resulting in roughly 54 lines per page and translating to one minute of screen time.

  • Scene Heading (Slugline): Left margin (1.5"), UPPERCASE, double-spaced before, single-spaced after.
  • Action Lines: Left margin (1.5"), full width to right margin (1.0"), written in present tense.
  • Character Names: Centered at 3.7" from page left (or 2.2" from left margin), UPPERCASE.
  • Parentheticals: Indented to 3.1" from page left (or 1.6" from left margin), lowercase, wrapped in parentheses.
  • Dialogue Blocks: Indented to 2.5" from page left, maximum 3.5 inches wide (extending to 6.0").
  • Transitions: Right-aligned at 6.0" from page left, UPPERCASE (e.g., CUT TO:).

Beginning writers often view formatting as pedantic bureaucratic nonsense. They ask: "If my story is brilliant, why does it matter if my dialogue margin is half an inch off?"

It matters because a screenplay is not a novel meant for casual reading. A screenplay is a technical architectural blueprint designed to estimate production budgets, coordinate shooting schedules, and measure film runtime.

Under the universal 1-page-equals-1-minute calculation, a 110-page script indicates a 110-minute feature film. If your margins are loose or your font is Arial, an assistant director cannot schedule scene eighths, and a producer cannot budget day shoots. Formatting compliance is the baseline proof that you respect the production pipeline.

Element Encyclopedia

The Core Technical Elements Explained

1. The Slugline (Scene Heading)

Must contain three pieces of information: Interior/Exterior (INT. or EXT.), the Specific Location, and the Time of Day (DAY, NIGHT, DUSK, DAWN). Never write specific hours like "INT. OFFICE - 3:15 PM." The camera only cares about lighting conditions.

INT. METROPOLITAN BANK VAULT - NIGHT

2. Action Paragraphs (The White Space Rule)

Action lines describe what the camera sees and what the microphone hears in present tense. Professional writers strictly follow the 4-line rule: break dense paragraphs into 2-to-3 line clusters. Blocks of text thicker than 4 lines look like novels and slow the reader's eye.

She drops the satchel. The leather tears on the curb.

3. Parentheticals (Wrylies)

Parentheticals describe attitude, action, or inflection during dialogue. Use them sparingly. Never explain emotions that the dialogue already conveys. Parentheticals must be lower-case, enclosed in parentheses, and placed directly between the character name and dialogue line.

(whispering under breath)

4. Extensions (V.O. vs. O.S.)

Appears immediately beside the Character Name in parentheses. (V.O.) stands for Voice-Over (the speaker is not physically present in the scene, like a documentary narrator). (O.S.) stands for Off-Screen (the character is physically in the room, but outside the camera's visual frame, like shouting from another room).

DETECTIVE REYES (O.S.) or NARRATOR (V.O.)

The 5-Point Spec Script Submission Audit

Verify every page against these criteria before exporting to PDF for managers or festivals:

AUDIT 01
Is the font Courier 12pt (or Courier Prime)? Never use Courier New, Arial, Times New Roman, or custom fonts. Non-Courier fonts invalidate page-to-runtime estimation.
AUDIT 02
Is the left margin exactly 1.5 inches? Standard binders and three-hole punches require 1.5 inches so words on the left edge are not obscured.
AUDIT 03
Are unfilmable thoughts pruned from action lines? You cannot photograph: "He remembers the summer he lost his dog." Write only what an actor can physically do or say.
AUDIT 04
Are camera angles removed from the spec draft? Delete phrases like "THE CAMERA PANS" or "ANGLE ON." That is the director's job. Direct the eye through description order.
AUDIT 05
Does every scene break cleanly at page transitions? Never leave an orphan Character Name at the bottom of a page with dialogue appearing on the next. Use proper (MORE) / (CONT'D) pagination.
