How to End a Story: 6 Powerful Ending Types for Scripts & Videos

A hook convinces an audience to start watching, but the ending determines whether they ever return. Discover how to construct final scenes that deliver emotional resonance, answer the dramatic argument, and leave a permanent imprint.

BY DRAFTSRUSH STUDIO • UPDATED FOR 2026 • 8 MIN READ
TL;DR // The Resolution Framework

How to End a Story Effectively

A story ends when the protagonist makes an irreversible choice during the climax that permanently resolves the central dramatic argument, transitioning the narrative into a new equilibrium where the character or world is fundamentally changed.

  • The Resolved Ending: Closes all primary and secondary plot lines definitively with zero unanswered narrative questions.
  • The Unresolved Ending: Provides emotional catharsis while leaving the literal physical outcome or moral debate open to the audience.
  • The Circular Ending: Returns the protagonist to the opening setting or frame, visually highlighting their internal transformation.
  • The Twist Ending: Introduces an earned, seeded revelation that completely re-contextualizes every prior scene in hindsight.
  • The Bittersweet Ending: The protagonist wins their internal moral need, but permanently sacrifices their external physical desire.
  • The Expanded Horizon Ending: Resolves the immediate plot arc while revealing the world is much larger, establishing higher future stakes.

An audience will forgive an awkward first act or a slow sequence in Act 2 if the climax and resolution stick the landing. But if your ending fizzles out into a rushed convenience or unearned sentimentality, the entire narrative feels wasted.

An ending is not simply the moment the plot stops moving. An ending is the delivery of the story's controlling idea. It is the proof that the character's journey mattered, that choices had costs, and that the world was irrevocably altered.

Here are the six fundamental ways to conclude a narrative, complete with real screenplay examples and practical techniques for video essayists.

Ending Type 01

The Resolved (Closed) Ending

COMPLETE CLOSURE

The closed ending ties up every narrative thread. The central mystery is solved, the villain is captured or killed, and the romantic leads unite or part ways forever. There are zero remaining unanswered questions.

Hallmark Films: The Shawshank Redemption, Casablanca, Die Hard
Primary Emotion: Catharsis, justice, and absolute finality

How to Execute It

Ensure the resolution is brought about directly by the protagonist's active choices, never external coincidence. If an outside force saves the protagonist in the final five pages, the closure feels hollow.

Ending Type 02

The Unresolved (Ambiguous) Ending

INTELLECTUAL RESIDUAL

An ambiguous ending provides emotional closure while deliberately leaving the literal outcome open to interpretation. The story stops precisely at the moment of decision, leaving the audience to debate what happened.

Hallmark Films: Inception (spinning top), The Thing (freezing in the snow)
Primary Emotion: Lingering philosophical curiosity and debate

How to Execute It

Ambiguity is not lazy writing. You must supply enough evidence for both competing outcomes to feel valid. If the audience feels you simply did not know how to finish the script, they will resent the ambiguity.

Ending Type 03

The Circular (Bookend) Ending

THEMATIC MEASUREMENT

The circular ending returns the character to the exact physical space, image, or situation where the story began. The surroundings are identical, but the protagonist has fundamentally transformed.

Hallmark Films: Cast Away (the crossroads), Gone Girl (the opening/closing head shot)
Primary Value: Visual contrast between who they were and who they became

How to Execute It

Use Blake Snyder's "Opening Image vs Final Image" formula. The final image should mirror the opening frame, providing visual proof of internal character transformation.

Ending Type 04

The Twist Ending

COGNITIVE REALIGNMENT

The twist ending subverts audience expectations by introducing a hidden truth that reframes the entire preceding narrative. It forces the audience to rethink every line of dialogue they just heard.

Hallmark Films: The Sixth Sense, The Others, and our breakdown of Se7en's "What's in the Box?" scene
The Golden Rule: Surprise in the moment, inevitable in hindsight

How to Execute It

Plant clues in plain sight. If a viewer re-watches the project, every single scene must hold up under the new revelation without creating plot holes.

Ending Type 05

The Bittersweet Ending

REALISTIC SACRIFICE

In a bittersweet ending, the protagonist achieves what they needed morally, but loses what they wanted physically (or vice versa). It pairs emotional satisfaction with profound melancholy.

Hallmark Films: La La Land, Chinatown, and the climax in our Whiplash final drum solo breakdown
Primary Value: Deep emotional truth and maturity

How to Execute It

Avoid pure tragedy or pure triumph. Make sure the audience recognizes that the character grew stronger, even if the price of that growth was permanently heartbreaking.

Ending Type 06

The Expanded Horizon Ending

FRANCHISE & WORLD EXPANSION

The immediate conflict is resolved, but the final moments reveal that this conflict was merely the first skirmish in a much larger, ongoing war. The camera pulls back to reveal a wider horizon.

Hallmark Films: Batman Begins (Joker card), The Matrix (Neo flying above the city)
Primary Value: Momentum and future narrative anticipation

How to Execute It

Do not leave the current story half-finished just to tease a sequel. Complete the current arc first, then provide a glimpse into the larger world that awaits.

The 3-Question Resolution Audit

Before finalizing the last five pages of your screenplay or the closing minute of your video essay, evaluate your ending with these criteria:

AUDIT 01
Did the protagonist make the decisive move? If an external secondary character or accidental twist saves the protagonist, rewrite the climax so your main character takes the final action.
AUDIT 02
Was there a permanent cost? If victory was free and easy, the ending feels unearned. Highlight the sacrifice required to achieve the resolution.
AUDIT 03
Does the final visual contrast the opening? Never end on an arbitrary camera angle. Make your final image mirror and contrast your opening frame.
