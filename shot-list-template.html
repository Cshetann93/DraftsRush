02 / The fundamentals

What is a shot list?

A shot list is a written plan of the individual camera shots a production needs to capture. It turns a screenplay scene or video concept into a practical filming plan, so the director, cinematographer, and crew can agree on what to shoot before the camera rolls.

A useful film shot list usually records the scene number, shot number, framing, camera angle, movement, action or composition, sound requirements, equipment, and production notes. The exact fields depend on the scale of the shoot: a small interview may need only a few columns, while a narrative short film may require detailed continuity and coverage notes.

Shot list vs. storyboard

A shot list explains the planned shots in words and production details. A storyboard visualizes those shots as drawings or frames. Use a shot list to organize and communicate the plan; add storyboards when composition, blocking, or complex action needs a visual reference.

03 / Step-by-step

How to make a shot list for a film

01

Break the script into scenes

Start with the screenplay or shooting script. Identify each scene's location, time of day, characters, key action, and story purpose. If you are planning a video rather than a film, break the outline into setups or beats.

02

Decide what the audience must see

List the essential story information, reactions, props, and visual details. Build coverage around the dramatic point of the scene rather than adding camera angles without a purpose.

03

Choose framing and camera angle

Use wide shots to establish space, medium shots to show action and interaction, and close-ups to emphasize reactions or details. Choose high, low, eye-level, or overhead angles based on what the moment should communicate.

04

Add movement, audio, and equipment

Note whether the camera stays still, pans, tracks, or moves closer. Record dialogue, room tone, sound effects, lens choices, rigs, or special equipment that the crew needs to prepare.

05

Check continuity and practical constraints

Check screen direction, eyelines, props, wardrobe, lighting changes, location access, available time, and equipment. Group shots by location or setup when it helps production, while protecting the story's continuity.

06

Share the list and update it on set

Make sure the director and camera team understand the priorities. Mark shots as planned, completed, or needing another take. Treat the list as a working document: adjust it when the location, schedule, or performance changes.

What should a shot list include?

AudioDialogue, room tone, effects, wild tracks, or sound details to capture.
Lens / gearLens, tripod, dolly, gimbal, lighting needs, or specialist equipment when known.
Notes / statusContinuity reminders, priority, safety notes, and whether the shot is complete.

05 / Avoid the common traps

Shot list mistakes to avoid

  • Planning angles without story purpose. Every setup should reveal information, clarify action, shape emotion, or support the edit.
  • Forgetting reaction shots and inserts. Small details, listening reactions, and cutaways can be essential to making a scene work in the edit.
  • Ignoring continuity. Eyelines, screen direction, hand positions, props, and actor movement should match across coverage.
  • Overcomplicating the plan. Build a realistic list for the available time, crew, location, and gear. Mark must-have shots separately from optional coverage.
  • Leaving audio out. Visual planning is not enough when dialogue, room tone, ambience, or specific sound effects are needed.

07 / Quick answers

Shot list template FAQ

What is a shot list in filmmaking?

It is a written plan of the camera shots needed for a scene or production, including framing, angle, movement, action, sound, and practical notes.

How many shots should a short film have?

There is no fixed number. It depends on scene complexity, pacing, performance, coverage, and production resources. Plan the shots needed to tell the story clearly rather than aiming for a target count.

Can I use a shot list for YouTube videos or interviews?

Yes. The same template works for YouTube, interviews, product videos, music videos, student films, and short films. Customize the columns for the format.

How do I download the shot list?

Fill in the worksheet and select Export CSV to open it in a spreadsheet, or choose Print / Save PDF to print a copy or save it as a PDF from your browser.
