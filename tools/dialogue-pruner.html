Craft Mechanics

Why Dialogue Pruning Protects Audience Retention

The Fatal Flaw of Conversational Filler

When writers draft conversations, they naturally mimic real-world speech patterns. Real people hesitate, clear their throats, say "hello," exchange pleasantries, and summarize points they just made. In casual conversation, this is polite social lubrication. In a YouTube video essay, short film, or TikTok drama, conversational filler is fatal dead air.

Every unnecessary conversational tag—phrases like "Look," "As I said before," "To be honest with you," or "Let me tell you something"—adds 0.5 to 1.2 seconds of non-information to your timeline. When viewers listen on mobile, these micro-delays break the narrative rhythm and invite a quick thumb-swipe to the next video.

The Late-In, Early-Out Principle

Hollywood screenwriting operates on a foundational rule: enter the scene late, and leave early. The same rule applies directly to spoken dialogue lines. Pruning dialogue forces you to cut the greeting and jump directly into the ideological conflict, emotional reveal, or counter-argument.

If Character A accuses Character B of embezzlement, do not write two introductory lines about the weather. Start with the accusation. When a line is pruned of passive throat-clearing, the pacing tightens, actors deliver words with greater physical impact, and your average view duration (AVD) climbs significantly across all video formats.

The 3 Rules of Tight Spoken Dialogue

1. Ban Greetings

Never script "Hi," "How are you," or phone introductions unless someone gets hung up on mid-word.

2. Cut Obvious Answers

If a character asks a yes/no question, eliminate the "Yes." Have them state the immediate next consequence instead.

3. The 12-Word Ceiling

In dynamic video, lines exceeding 12 words turn dialogue into speeches. Split long sentences across visual cuts.
