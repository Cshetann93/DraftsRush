Production Manual

How 1st ADs & Line Producers Break Down a Script

Before a single camera rolls, the screenplay must be transformed into physical logistics. An assistant director reads the script with colored highlighters, tagging every physical item that costs money or requires crew setup.

The most critical metric is Page Eighths. In Hollywood, a standard 8.5 x 11-inch screenplay page is divided into eight one-inch segments. An experienced crew shoots roughly 4 to 5 pages per day on an indie film (or 2 pages on a big-budget studio project). Counting eighths ensures you never over-schedule a shooting day.

Frequently Asked Questions: Script Breakdown

What is the difference between set dressing and a prop?

If an actor touches or physically interacts with an object (like picking up a vintage telephone), it is classified as a Prop. If the telephone sits untouched on an office desk in the background, it is classified as Set Dressing.

How many eighths can you schedule in one production day?

Standard indie film productions schedule between 24/8 and 36/8 (3 to 4.5 pages) per 12-hour shooting day. Dialogue-heavy single-location scenes can move faster, while complex action sequences with practical squibs or stunts may only cover 1 full page (8/8) per day.
